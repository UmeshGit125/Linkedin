Companion to `LinkedIn_Comment_Intelligence_System.md` (whose Category A–D targeting pulls from this file) and governed by the same discipline as every other file in the system: **real research only, "could not verify" beats a plausible guess, and never invent a creator, a follower count, a metric, a quote, or a result.**

## Section 0 — Method (how an entry gets added)

1. **Source priority:** LinkedIn > YouTube > X > podcast > newsletter > personal website > secondary summary. The closer to the creator's own words, the better.
2. **Separate the four things, every time:** VERIFIED FACT / ANALYSIS / INFERENCE / OPPORTUNITY.
3. **Assign a confidence level:** HIGH (primary source, directly observable, recent) / MEDIUM (secondary source or partial evidence) / LOW (inference, undated, or single weak source). If it can't be checked, write **"could not verify"** — not a number.
4. **Never estimate a metric.** Follower counts, revenue, client names, engagement numbers: if not directly visible in a primary source, they are not recorded.
5. **No record from memory.** A creator read "a while ago" is not a verified entry.
6. **Research is manual.** No scraping or bulk extraction, consistent with the account-wide policy that research and commenting stay human-verified.
7. **Re-verify before reuse.** A creator's facts can change; an entry older than **TBD** (review window not yet set) gets re-checked before being cited in a post.

## Section 4 — How to study a creator without copying

**The Creator Breakdown Rule: creator content is a source of insight, never a source of wording.**

- Read for the **decision underneath** the post — what problem they hit, what they chose, what it cost them — not for the sentence structure.
- Extract the idea; apply it to work you actually did. If it doesn't connect to anything real, it isn't a post yet.
- **Never reuse phrasing.** Similar sentence rhythm copied from a creator is plagiarism with extra steps.
- **Always attribute when you build on someone's specific idea**, and link/credit the original.
- **Never present their results as yours.** If their number is the evidence, it's their number and it's labelled as theirs.

BAD: take a creator's post, keep the structure, swap in AI synonyms, publish.
BETTER: take the underlying idea, describe your own angle from your own project, and label anything without evidence as TBD.

## Section 8 — What not to do

- Don't add a name just to have something in the table.
- Don't follow someone because they're popular — follow them because they're relevant.
- Don't treat Tier 2 technique creators as subject-matter sources, or Tier 1 practitioners as writing-style models.
- Don't cite an entry in a post if its confidence is LOW or its facts aren't re-verified.
- Don't pad this file with generic categories instead of real people — an empty table is correct until real research exists.
